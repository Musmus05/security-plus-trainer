## Why this objective matters

Vulnerability management is not a synonym for running a scanner. On the exam, a finding moves
through a small operational system: discover it, decide whether it matters here, choose and carry
out a response, prove the response worked, and communicate the remaining risk. Questions usually
test the boundary between those activities. A scan finding is not yet a remediation decision; a
patch installed is not yet proof that exposure is gone.

<KeyPoint>
  Read the verb in a scenario. **Finding** a weakness is identification. Deciding its importance is
  analysis. Changing the environment is remediation. Scanning or testing again is validation. Giving
  leaders the trend, risk and exceptions is reporting.
</KeyPoint>

## Identification methods: build a credible list

The process needs an asset context before it needs a tool. A vulnerability report that says an IP
address is affected is less useful than one that identifies the owner, service, operating system,
data sensitivity, and internet exposure. Asset inventory and ownership let a team tell whether a
result is a production payment server, an unused test machine, or an address no longer assigned.

| Identification method | What it is good at                                                    | Important limitation                                              |
| --------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Vulnerability scan    | Comparing discovered services and software with known weaknesses      | Its result can be incomplete, stale, or a false positive          |
| Authenticated scan    | Inspecting a host with approved credentials                           | It needs scoped, protected credentials and host access            |
| Unauthenticated scan  | Seeing the target as an external attacker might                       | It cannot reliably see every installed component or setting       |
| Manual review         | Checking a configuration, design, or compensating control             | It is slower and depends on the reviewer’s evidence               |
| Penetration test      | Demonstrating whether a weakness can be exploited within agreed rules | A time-limited test is not a complete vulnerability inventory     |
| Threat intelligence   | Adding information about active exploitation, actors, or indicators   | It prioritizes known information; it does not inspect every asset |

A **vulnerability scan** is a systematic check for known conditions, such as exposed services,
missing updates, weak configuration, or vulnerable software versions. It may use network probing, an
agent, or credentials. An **authenticated scan** can inspect installed packages and local policy,
whereas an **unauthenticated scan** tests what can be observed without logging in. Neither is
automatically better: the first provides depth and the second provides an attacker-facing view.

<ExamTrap>
  Do not call every security test a penetration test. A scanner identifies possible weaknesses; a
  penetration test attempts controlled exploitation to establish impact. They complement each other,
  but a successful scan does not prove exploitation and an unsuccessful test does not prove that
  every vulnerability is absent.
</ExamTrap>

Discovery also comes from vendor advisories, bug reports, configuration reviews, log investigation,
and threat intelligence. **Threat intelligence** matters when it changes priority: a vulnerability
that is actively exploited, reachable from the internet, or associated with the organisation’s
technology deserves attention sooner than an equally scored but isolated issue. Keep the source,
timestamp, affected asset, and evidence with the finding so the next stage has something to assess.

## Analysis: turn a finding into a decision

Analysis answers two different questions: _is the finding real?_ and _what does it mean in this
environment?_ A scanner can misidentify a version, miss a compensating control, or report a
condition already removed. Checking the asset, configuration, version, and exposure prevents a team
from spending outage time on a false positive. Conversely, a result should not be dismissed merely
because the scanner cannot demonstrate an exploit.

| Factor                    | Why it changes priority                                                                   |
| ------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Severity                  | A higher technical impact can justify faster action, but is not the only factor           |
| Exploitability            | Public exploits, low complexity, or active exploitation raise urgency                     |
| Exposure                  | An internet-facing or broadly reachable service has more opportunity for attack           |
| Asset criticality         | A system supporting sensitive data or a critical business process has greater consequence |
| Compensating controls     | Segmentation, filtering, monitoring, or restricted access can reduce practical risk       |
| Dependencies and downtime | A fix may need a restart, coordinated change, or testing of connected systems             |

Severity scores help compare technical characteristics, but vulnerability management is risk-based
work. A medium-severity issue on a public administrator interface may outrank a critical issue on a
retired, disconnected laboratory host. Record the reasoning: ownership, business impact, exposure,
evidence, target date, and the person accepting any temporary exception. That record makes a queue
defensible rather than just long.

<Mnemonic>
  **Known is not equal to urgent; exposed and exploitable is urgent.** Start with the weakness, then
  add the asset, path to it, business consequence, and controls around it.
</Mnemonic>

## Vulnerability response and remediation

Once analysed, the team chooses a response. **Remediation** removes or corrects the weakness: apply
a vendor patch, change an insecure configuration, replace unsupported software, rotate a leaked
credential, or disable an unnecessary service. A change should still follow the organisation’s
change process: test where possible, schedule a maintenance window, document a rollback path, and
notify affected owners.

Sometimes immediate remediation is impossible. A legacy application may have no patch, a critical
service may require extensive regression testing, or a vendor fix may not yet exist. A
**compensating control** reduces exposure without claiming to remove the underlying weakness. Useful
examples include network segmentation, stricter access control, web filtering, an application allow
list, additional monitoring, or isolating the system. These controls are risk treatment, not
evidence that the vulnerability disappeared.

| Response                             | Appropriate outcome                                                                               |
| ------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Remediate                            | The root weakness is removed or corrected                                                         |
| Mitigate with a compensating control | Exposure or likelihood is reduced while the weakness remains                                      |
| Accept risk                          | An authorized owner knowingly accepts documented residual risk for a period                       |
| Transfer risk                        | A contract or insurance arrangement shifts some financial consequence, not the technical weakness |
| Avoid risk                           | The vulnerable service or activity is retired, disabled, or replaced                              |

<InPractice>
  A team discovers a vulnerable library in an internet-facing service. It first confirms that the
  deployed version is affected, checks whether the vulnerable feature is enabled, and assesses the
  service’s exposure. If the patch needs testing, it can temporarily limit access at the reverse
  proxy and increase monitoring. The patch is still scheduled; the proxy rule is not a reason to
  forget the finding.
</InPractice>

## Validation of remediation: prove the outcome

Validation asks whether the intended condition exists **after** the change. Rerunning the relevant
scan is common, but it may not be sufficient. Verify the updated version or configuration, confirm
the service restarted when required, test the control from the relevant network path, and check that
the application still works. A patch on disk may not protect a process that is still running old
code in memory.

The validation method should match the original evidence. If a credentialed scan found a missing
package update, repeat an authenticated check. If the finding was an exposed management page,
confirm it is no longer reachable from an unauthorized network. If a compensating control was used,
validate the control and leave the original vulnerability open in tracking until it is actually
remediated or formally accepted.

<ExamTrap>
  Closing a ticket because a change was performed is not validation. The ticket can close when
  evidence shows the finding is fixed, mitigated as documented, or accepted by the right owner.
</ExamTrap>

## Reporting: make risk actionable

Reporting serves different readers. Engineers need affected assets, reproduction evidence, owners,
due dates, and validation status. Management needs trends, overdue critical findings, exposure,
exceptions, and whether remediation capacity matches the risk. A useful report distinguishes new,
open, remediated, mitigated, accepted, and false-positive findings; otherwise a falling total might
only mean that tickets were closed without proof.

Metrics can include remediation time by severity, percentage of assets covered by scanning, number
of overdue findings, recurring configuration issues, and validated closure rate. Metrics do not
replace judgement. Their purpose is to reveal whether the programme is finding assets, resolving the
most important exposure, and carrying documented residual risk.

## Test yourself

1. A scan says a server is vulnerable, but the owner says the service was removed. What activity
   should happen before a patch is scheduled?
2. A vendor patch cannot be applied for two weeks. What response reduces exposure without removing
   the vulnerability?
3. What makes a remediation ticket ready to close?

<KeyPoint>
  (1) Analysis: verify the finding, asset state, and exposure. (2) A compensating control, such as
  segmentation or stricter filtering, plus documented follow-up. (3) Validation evidence showing the
  weakness is fixed or its approved treatment is operating as documented.
</KeyPoint>
